2 · What would have been simpler with ONE agent + tools?

Ticket 102 is the clearest example of a problem that could have been handled by a single competent agent with the right tools. A single agent with access to: - get_rent_payment_context - get_cash_position - get_payment_history - the ability to prepare a payment proposal could have handled almost the entire reasoning process. The workflow is relatively deterministic: verify the rent obligation -> compare the due date with the shop date -> check available cash -> prepare the payment -> wait for human approval There is little room for genuine inter-agent negotiation. Facilities and Accounting represent different business functions, but for this specific ticket their tasks were closely connected and relatively straightforward. Ticket 103 could also have been simpler with one agent. A single commercial decision-support agent could have reviewed the pricing economics, checked inventory availability, identified that there was no approved discount policy, and escalated the decision to a human. However, I do not think this means that separating the agents was unnecessary. Even when one agent could technically perform the work, separating roles can create better governance and clearer responsibilities. For example, Accounting can focus on financial decisions, Inventory on operational constraints, and Customer Service on external communication. This separation becomes more valuable as the system grows and agents receive more tools and authority. It can provide specialization, segregation of duties, and a clearer audit trail. My main takeaway is that there is a trade-off: For simple and deterministic tasks, one agent with good tools may be faster and easier to operate. For more complex workflows, especially when different types of authority or expertise are involved, separate agents can provide better governance and make the system easier to expand.

3 · What other problems could this agent team solve?

Problem 1

Decide whether an upcoming invoice can be paid safely

The system could evaluate whether the shop has enough cash to pay an upcoming invoice without creating a negative balance. Accounting could use the existing payment history, cash position, and invoice/vendor context to verify the obligation and determine whether the payment is affordable. If appropriate, it could then prepare a payment proposal for human approval.
Problem 2

Handle another customer order with partial inventory

The team could also handle a customer order where only part of the requested quantity is currently available. Inventory could use get_customer_order_fulfillment_context, get_product_availability, and the vendor-status tools to identify the exact inventory shortfall and determine whether replenishment is possible. If a vendor payment or purchase is required, Accounting could evaluate the cash implications and prepare the relevant proposal for human approval. Customer Service could then draft a response based on the verified fulfillment situation.
Problem 3

Payment-control audit

The existing system could also be used as an internal control before payments are approved. Accounting could use get_payment_history() and get_cash_position() to check whether a proposed payment has already been made and whether enough cash exists. Facilities or Inventory could validate the underlying obligation, such as a rent payment or vendor invoice, using the existing context and status tools. The Boss could then surface only legitimate and properly supported payments for human approval. This is different from simply deciding whether the shop can afford a payment: the objective is to identify duplicate, unnecessary, or inconsistent payments before money leaves the account.

4 · Three problems that would exceed the current team

Problem 1

Supplier selection

The current system cannot reliably select the best supplier for a product when the relationship between a SKU and its possible vendors is not available in the database. To solve this problem, I would need new tools that allow Inventory to identify suppliers for a specific SKU and compare their availability and lead times. Accounting would also need access to vendor quotes or costs so it could compare the financial alternatives. The Boss could then combine the operational and financial information and make a recommendation. Possible new capabilities could include: - supplier-by-SKU lookup - vendor quotes - vendor lead times - vendor performance or reliability history
Problem 2

Giving a committed delivery date

The current team cannot confidently communicate a specific arrival date for a product, because a vendor lead time is not the same thing as a confirmed shipment or delivery date. To solve this, Inventory would need tools that provide actual logistics information, such as purchase-order status, confirmed ship dates, shipment tracking, and expected arrival dates. Inventory could own and verify these logistics facts, and Customer Service could then communicate a delivery estimate supported by real shipment information rather than only a generic vendor lead time. Possible new capabilities could include: - purchase-order status - confirmed ship date - shipment tracking - expected receipt date
Problem 3

Forward-looking liquidity planning

The current team cannot properly answer whether the shop will be able to afford rent, invoices, payroll, and planned inventory purchases over the next seven days. The current tools are useful for evaluating today's cash position and individual obligations, but they do not provide a complete forward-looking cash-flow forecast. To solve this problem, Accounting would need tools that combine expected cash inflows, scheduled payments, payroll, recurring expenses, and planned purchases into a projected cash balance. The Boss could then use that forecast to decide between competing uses of limited cash and escalate important trade-offs to a human when necessary. Possible new capabilities could include: - scheduled future payments - expected cash inflows - payroll obligations - planned inventory purchases - projected cash balance over a selected time horizon
